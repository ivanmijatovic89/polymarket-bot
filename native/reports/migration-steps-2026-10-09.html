    At the end

    The finished system. Rust owns everything that happens inside one market. TypeScript keeps everything around it and talks to the engine through one job contract: a market job in, a result out.

    Rust owns

    • Reading recorded markets (V4 packages, later Telonex history) and live market data
    • Order books, the event loop, order state, cash and positions
    • The strategy SDK, plugins, and the strategies written for it
    • The simulator, built from measurements of the real exchange
    • The exchange adapter: signing, placing, cancelling, the user feed, the journal
    • Paper mode and, last, the live runtime with its guards
    • Per-market statistics, traces and reports

    TypeScript keeps

    • The CLI and the producer: which markets, which params, enqueue
    • Market selection and every MySQL write; the dashboard; the fleet queue and worker shim
    • Recorder V4 and the dataset pipelines (Telonex, Binance, Chainlink)
    • On-chain work: split, merge, redeem, approvals, pUSD
    • The rules capture, the WebUI server, Global Runtime, research tools
    • Today's TypeScript strategies, on the TypeScript engine, until protocols write Rust ones
    Three things to hold on to.
    1. Only N1 and N2 are defined. N3 is written from your probe results. N4 to N9 are a roadmap and get written one at a time.
    2. You never approve a plan. You look at a finished step: a replayed market, an order in the journal, a simulator-versus-probes table.
    3. Nothing from the old TypeScript simulator is copied. Every fill, fee and delay in the Rust simulator comes from a measurement or is marked as unmeasured.
