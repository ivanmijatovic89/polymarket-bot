# DECISIONS — design forks and why

Each entry: context → options → decision → rejected-and-why.
Newest at the bottom.

---

## D1 — One operating role (Scientist) + fresh-context Judge, not a multi-role split

**Context.** The old system splits work across ProposeFamily / Researcher
roles with stage-gate documents. The failure mode a role split guards against
is self-graded homework: the session that built a strategy also judges its
results and is motivated (by narrative momentum, not intent) to see an edge.

**Options.** (a) Mirror the two-role split; (b) one Scientist role, with
mechanical bias protection (pre-registration + frozen decision rules) and a
fresh-context Judge subagent that issues verdicts from the spec + results
alone; (c) fully autonomous single role, self-judging.

**Decision.** (b). The protection that actually works is structural, not
organizational: the decision rule is written and committed *before* results
exist, and the entity applying it has no memory of building the strategy.
A fresh-context subagent gives exactly that property at near-zero cost, per
`docs/reference/prompting-claude-fable-5.md` ("fresh-context verifier
subagents tend to outperform self-critique"). A second *session role* adds
coordination surface (locks, handoffs, session scheduling) without adding
protection beyond what the fresh-context Judge already provides.

**Rejected.** (a) — coordination overhead and context fragmentation; the role
boundary is enforced socially rather than mechanically. (c) — self-judging is
the exact bias channel the charter says to protect against.

## D2 — Evidence thresholds as formulas (t-statistics), not fixed market counts

**Context.** The charter demands: "How much data buys how much belief?
Justify every threshold from first principles." Fixed counts ("run 500, then
3000") are cargo cult unless tied to effect size.

**Decision.** Express every belief threshold as a t-statistic on per-market
PnL: t = (mean/std) · √N = quality · √N, where quality is exactly the
engine's persisted `qualitySystem` (mean/std over all markets, skipped = 0).
Stage sizes are then *derived*: the N needed to resolve the minimum effect
size worth trading at the required t. Defaults are recorded, but the formula
is the rule; the defaults are recomputed when the measured cost structure
changes. Full derivation in `protocol/EPISTEMOLOGY.md`.

**Rejected.** Fixed counts without power analysis — they either waste compute
on effects too small to matter or pass noise on effects the sample cannot
resolve. Also rejected: bootstrap/bayesian machinery beyond the t-frame for
v1 — per-market PnL across thousands of independent 15m episodes is close to
the iid case where the t-statistic is honest; sophistication can come later
and would slow the loop tonight.

## D3 — Time-split holdout, fixed at registration, one-shot

**Context.** Grading on the data used for iteration overfits. Need an
out-of-sample discipline that survives the fact that one agent controls
everything.

**Options.** (a) Random market split; (b) interleaved calendar split (even/odd
weeks); (c) time split: exploration = older markets, holdout = the most
recent block, boundary frozen inside the pre-registered spec.

**Decision.** (c). The objective is *durable* edge — the failure that kills
live deployment is regime drift, and only a chronological holdout tests
survival into data that is genuinely "later" than everything iterated on.
The boundary (a specific `market_start_ms`) is written into the experiment
spec at registration; the holdout run happens once, after the exploration
verdict, and its result is final for that strategy version. Re-rolling the
holdout after seeing it = new strategy version, new experiment, and the old
holdout becomes exploration data (it is burned).

**Rejected.** (a) random split — leaks regime: adjacent 15m windows from the
same day land on both sides, so the "holdout" is not out-of-sample in the
sense that matters. (b) interleaving — same leak, milder; also complicates
extend-based coverage growth, which is contiguous-by-time in the engine
(`--extend` grows from the covered edge).

## D4 — The registry is append-only files; runs are labeled, never renamed

**Context.** Files are the only memory that survives a session. The engine's
own durable records are `backtest_runs` rows keyed by `batch_uid` label and
immutable `cmd`.

**Decision.** One directory `protocol/registry/experiments/` with one file
per experiment (`EXP-NNN-slug.md`), append-only sections (spec first, then
run records, then verdicts). `batch_uid` for every run is the experiment id
plus stage (`EXP-012-probe`), so DB rows and registry files cross-link
without a lookup table. An `INDEX.md` table (one line per experiment) is
regenerated by a tool, never hand-edited.

**Rejected.** A JSON/DB-backed registry — harder for a fresh session to skim,
and the DB is the *engine's* memory, not the protocol's; mixing them couples
protocol state to schema migrations. Rejected renaming/moving experiment
files on status change — breaks cross-links from DB comments and old
sessions' notes.

## D5 — Mechanism-first idea ledger; dedupe by mechanism, not by indicator

**Context.** Idea generation is cheap for an LLM; the scarce resource is
compute and holdout data. Unstructured idea lists degenerate into re-testing
the same mechanism under different indicator names.

**Decision.** Every idea entry must state: (1) the *mechanism* — who is on
the other side of the trade and why they systematically lose (in one of a
small closed set of mechanism classes, e.g. "stale quotes after fast moves",
"overpriced tails near expiry", "spread capture in quiet regimes",
"UP/DOWN sum mispricing"); (2) a falsifiable prediction about *recorded*
data; (3) the cheapest experiment that could kill it. Two ideas with the
same mechanism class and the same prediction target are duplicates, whatever
the parameterization. Priority = expected information per compute-hour:
mechanism novelty × plausibility × (1 / test cost).

**Rejected.** Indicator-first ideation ("try RSI", "try Bollinger") — it
multiplies parameter cells without multiplying mechanisms, which is exactly
the curve-fit target the charter forbids.

## D6 — Simulator-bias classification is a mandatory verdict field

**Context.** The engine study (CAPABILITIES.md §4) shows the simulator is
knowably optimistic in specific ways (full-size maker fills on touch-through,
zero market impact, free instant split/merge, costless settlement) and
knowably pessimistic in others (worst-queue, 156 bps taker fee on every
taker fill).

**Decision.** Every verdict must classify where the measured edge sits
relative to these biases: a strategy whose PnL is dominated by maker fills
in thin books, or by large size, is flagged `simulator-favored` and cannot
pass confirmation on backtest evidence alone (it needs a live paper phase —
out of scope tonight, but recorded as the required next step). This is a
field in the spec template so the judge cannot skip it.

**Rejected.** Applying a numeric "haircut" to maker PnL — any constant would
be an invented cost, which the charter forbids; classification + escalation
to live validation is honest about what backtests can and cannot prove.
