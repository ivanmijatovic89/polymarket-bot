### (b) Discrepancies

1. **EXP-006 verdict + E16: "62 decisive markets" is a misreading of the tool's notation.** `results.ts` prints `winRate(played)=0.453 (53/62)` where `(53/62)` is **wins/losses** (source: `wonLost: "${won}/${lost}"`, winRate = won/played). So run 336 has 53 wins + 62 losses = **115 decisive markets** (plus 2 played markets with pnl exactly 0), not 62. Consequences: the verdict's "62/500 = ~12.4% decisive" should be 115/500 = 23%, and E16's "win rate on decisive markets 0.453" is mislabeled — 0.453 is the win rate over all 117 played; over decisive it is 53/115 = 0.461. This error is conservative (23% is even further above the ~3% design-failure floor, and q/t/EV are unaffected), so the kill stands, but the number as written in both the verdict and E16 is wrong.
2. **Minor tool-level inconsistency in run 301 (not an author error, but worth recording):** results.ts reports winRate 0.9315 (10359/761 → 11120 outcomes over 11121 played), while entry-check.ts reports 0.9316 (10360/11121) — one played market with pnl exactly 0 is a loss-nor-win in one tool and a win in the other. Both documents quote their respective tool verbatim; the ±0.0001 does not affect any conclusion.
3. **E14 "every ask bucket sits on the diagonal" is a slight overstatement:** bucket 0.94-0.96 shows win rate 0.929 vs ~0.95 ask (n=438, ~1.7σ below diagonal). Within noise, but "on the diagonal" is a gloss, not the exact readout.

### (d) Logical gaps

- The E16/E17 "62 decisive" propagation (see b.1) is the only factual error found, and it biases *against* the strategy's escape hatch, not for the conclusion.
- EXP-003's kill is on t=−1.06 with the mechanical prediction technically holding (+0.0001) — the verdict itself flags this honestly; killing at the loose bar is per the registered rules, but E10's "strictly negative" phrasing rests on the fee arithmetic (zero gross − 156 bps = negative), which is arithmetically sound.
- E17's "EXHAUSTED" claim is properly scoped: it says *worst-queue-observable* edge space, explicitly carves out at-touch maker economics as unmeasurable, and distinguishes "fairly priced" (taker) from "negative" (maker punch-through). The maker-side generalization rests on exactly two parameter cells (one per regime), each killed at probe stage (N=500, t −1.5/−2.0) with no robustness neighborhood run — a kill-at-probe is per protocol, but "uniformly negative" across the regime axis is interpolated from two points, not a swept grid. That is a scope caveat, not a contradiction.

### (e) Overall verdict

The E9-E17 chain is empirically sound: every number I could re-derive from the database — all seven decisive run readouts (308, 309, 311, 312, 301-extended, 336, 342), the full EXP-001 robustness grid and latency curve (313-327), all four entry-check prediction tests, and all nine fill-feasibility counts (328, 331-341) — matches the registered documents at the displayed precision, the INDEX statuses match the verdicts, and every kill satisfies its experiment's own pre-registered kill bar as written. The single real error is the EXP-006 verdict's misreading of the wins/losses notation ("62 decisive" should be 115 decisive, win rate on decisive 0.461 not 0.453), which propagated into E16 but is conservative with respect to the kill decision; beyond that, the EXP-001 probe-stage numbers and E13/E15 diagnostics are unverifiable by construction (extended run, voided run, fixture output) though internally consistent. The "measured edge space is exhausted" conclusion is supported *as scoped* — taker mechanisms fairly priced net of fees on large samples, punch-through maker designs negative in both tested regimes — with the honest caveats that the maker side rests on two probe-stage cells at N=500 each and that the lessons themselves already concede at-touch maker economics were never measurable. I would correct the 62-vs-115 decisive-market figure in EXP-006's verdict record and E16, and otherwise accept the chain.
