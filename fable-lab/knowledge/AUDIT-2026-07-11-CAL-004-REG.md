- findings:

1. **(MINOR — bias protection / post-table degree of freedom)** The W-state envelope note (`fable-lab/knowledge/CALIBRATION-4.md:149-156`) forks on "a W candidate in a mid-priced bucket" (PARK) vs "T-state and extreme-price W-state candidates" (PROCEED to reserve), but never defines the mid/extreme boundary — no bucket enumeration, no numeric criterion. Why it matters: this is the only decision branch in the registration exercised AFTER seeing the table, and it governs spending the pristine reserve; a borderline W candidate (e.g. (850s, [0.90,0.98)), meanAsk ≈ 0.94, or anything in [0.80,0.90)) can be argued into either class post hoc. The problem is aggravated by finding 2: my outcome-free joint measurement shows W incidence at (850s, [0.90,0.98)) is 27.7-30.8% (UP nW=144/520, DOWN nW=159/516) — neither the "8-18%" the park rule quotes nor the "≳50%" the U45 envelope confirms, so the rule's own incidence rationale doesn't classify it. Evidence: CALIBRATION-4.md:149-156, 213-218; command output below (cell UP|850|7 tfr=0.723, DOWN|850|7 tfr=0.692). Fix: pre-read amendment freezing a mechanical criterion — either enumerate the buckets counted as extreme-price ([0.02,0.10), [0.90,0.98), [0.98,0.995], say), or better, freeze "PROCEED iff the reserve-projected bar at the cell's printed nW (3.75·se scaled by √(1/0.64)) ≤ the discovery point estimate; else PARK" so the tool's own printed table decides.

2. **(MINOR — statistical wording accuracy)** The claims "82-92% of samples uniformly across ALL 9 ask buckets" (CALIBRATION-4.md:29-31), "nT ≈ 0.82-0.92 × the CAL-001 cell n" (Power, :205), and "T cells share ~82-92% of their samples with the corresponding CAL-001 cell" (Dependence, :120-121) are true per (side × bucket) pooled over offsets — I reproduced tfr range 0.822-0.920 exactly — but false per cell: late-offset joint cells go well below (tfr 0.692-0.723 at 850s [0.90,0.98)), and correspondingly nW there is 0.28-0.31 × n, outside the "0.08-0.18" W range (:213). Why it matters: the seT multiplier "1.04-1.10" understates to ~1.18-1.20 for those cells, and the dependence/incidence wording will be quoted in the verdict. The disclosed pre-freeze inspection measured only the two marginals (spread×offset, spread×bucket — CALIBRATION-4.md:239-241), so the registration honestly didn't know the joint; but the wording asserts per-cell uniformity it never measured. Evidence: `node /tmp/cal4-audit-incidence.mjs` (outcome-free; asks/spreads only) — per-bucket tfr 0.822-0.920; cells UP|850|7 tfr=0.723 (nT=376,nW=144), DOWN|850|7 tfr=0.692 (nT=357,nW=159). Fix: pre-read wording amendment scoping the 82-92%/8-18% claims to the measured marginals and noting joint cells may deviate (the tool prints per-cell tfr anyway, so no tool change).

3. **(MINOR — non-derivable power figure, CAL-003 finding-2 class)** Reserve projection (CALIBRATION-4.md:219-226): "nT(reserve) ≈ 0.55 × nT(discovery)" is not derivable from the stated inputs. Under stationary tight fraction, the ratio is the market ratio 5,460/8,516 = 0.641 (both numerator and denominator are already T counts); 0.55 ≈ 0.641 × 0.855 looks like the tight fraction applied twice. Why it matters: the "≥ ~78% power" claim rests on it. Direction is conservative: with the correct 0.641, power at the discovery point estimate is tail(1.995 − 3.75·√0.641) ≈ 84.3%, vs 78.4% as stated — so confirmation feasibility is understated, not overstated, and no decision flips. Evidence: `python3` computation — ratio 0.55 → power 0.784 (matches the registration's 78%); ratio 0.641 → 0.843; z_0.023 = 1.995, 3.75/√(1/0.55) = 2.781 as the registration states. Fix: pre-read erratum deriving 0.641 and restating power ≈ 84% (or explicitly labeling 0.55 as deliberate attrition padding, if that was the intent).

4. **(MINOR — pipeline identity not bit-guaranteed, fail-safe)** calib4.ts computes gate-cell statistics by summing the T sub-cell then the W sub-cell (`statOf(grids[side][oi][bi])`, calib4.ts:315-338), whereas calib.ts sums the same observations interleaved in log order (calib.ts:185-196). FP addition is non-associative, so identity gates 5/6 (`toFixed(4)`/`toFixed(2)` string equality against PUB) are not mathematically guaranteed to reproduce. Why it matters: a mismatch would spuriously abort the one-shot (fail-safe direction — no silent wrong table is possible since winRate and n are integer-exact). I verified empirically, outcome-free, that it is harmless on the actual data: on all four gate cells the two summation orders differ by ~1e-13 in sumAsk / ~2e-14 in sumVar and agree exactly at printed precision (meanAsk toFixed(4) and se toFixed(4) identical). Evidence: `node /tmp/cal4-audit-fp.mjs` — UP|8 n=687 sumAsk diff 9.1e-13, meanAsk4 equal true, se4 equal true (all four cells). No fix required; worth a one-line note in the registration that gate reproduction is at printed precision, not bit-level, and an abort here means re-check summation order before suspecting the join.

5. **(MINOR — threshold derivation wording)** "one tick (0.01) at the venue's standard tick size plus a half-tick tolerance" (CALIBRATION-4.md:86-88; echoed calib4.ts:4): 0.0105 − 0.01 = 0.0005 is half of the FINE tick (0.001), not half of the stated standard tick (which would give 0.015). Why it matters: cosmetic only for validity — the constant is genuinely a-priori and robust (any tolerance in (0.0001, 0.0095) classifies standard-tick books identically, and 0.0005 cleanly separates a 0.010 from a 0.011 spread at fine tick; spreads at exactly 0.0105 cannot occur at either tick size, so the FP edge cases the selftest documents are unreachable on real data) — but the derivation as written doesn't produce the number, which is the kind of nick prior audits flagged. Fix: reword to "plus half of the fine tick (0.001) as FP tolerance".
